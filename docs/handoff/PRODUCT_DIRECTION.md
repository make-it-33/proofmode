# Product direction and durable intent

## Product thesis

ProofMode teaches people to direct, verify, recover from, and ship work with AI. The learner must practise judgment—not collect prompt tricks, watch passive lessons, or accept AI output because it sounds confident.

Core method:

`Source → AI move → Verification → Human decision → Outcome`

Core promise:

**Learn, practise, prove, and recover while remaining in control of the AI.**

ProofMode is a working product name. A rename is possible later, but must update product copy, routes where relevant, package/app metadata, legal/release assets, and handoff together.

## Audience

Primary: learners aged 13+, designed first for teenagers.

Also useful for students, unemployed learners, early-career builders, independent creators/developers, adults changing skills, and eventually teams. Teen-first means clear, motivating, safe, and visually credible—not childish. Employer/school products are separate future decisions and may not reuse consumer evidence without explicit consent, validity, privacy, and authorization design.

Under 13 remains unsupported. The product must never solicit a workaround, birthday, school, location, contact information, or identity merely to enforce the current boundary.

## Initial and future learning fields

V1 available field: **Agentic Coding**, beginner through advanced.

Future fields, only after the learning system is proven:

- Design with AI
- Business with AI
- Research with AI
- Game Development with AI

A field is not “available” until its curriculum, lessons, checkpoints, results, content QA, accessibility, safety, and support contract exist.

## What creates the advantage

- action and realistic practice instead of passive content;
- evidence and source checking instead of prompt folklore;
- explicit recovery from an AI mistake;
- a visible, replayable Proof Chain;
- broad professional desktop workspaces and focused mobile sequences;
- honest current/future states;
- privacy, youth safety, accessibility, and performance by design;
- clear, maintainable product/engineering specs;
- strong craft without fake users, stats, testimonials, or theatre.

Do not copy competitor feature lists. Win by making the learning loop more useful, inspectable, safe, and polished.

## No competition

Owner decision, 2026-08-28: remove competition from the active product and V1/V2 plans.

Do not implement:

- Arena or player-versus-player routes;
- ranks, ladders, leaderboards, seasons, leagues, or matchmaking;
- public score comparison or percentile pressure;
- prizes, wagering, challenge spam, or pay-to-win;
- social proof built from fabricated activity;
- streak loss, energy, guilt, or urgency mechanics.

Private challenge can mean a personal practice scenario; it must not imply another learner, a cohort, or ranking. Historical Arena/scoring records are superseded and retained only to preserve decision history. Reintroducing competition requires a new explicit owner decision and a material rewrite of product, youth-safety, fairness, privacy, moderation, architecture, and UX specifications.

## Website versus application

The public website is cinematic, explanatory, and acquisition oriented. The app is calm, operational, repeatable, and denser.

Website motion demonstrates product causality: AI claim → contradictory evidence → human intervention → corrected order → safer outcome. It does not use generic orbs, decorative 3D, fake terminal output, giant text that hides the app, autoplay audio, or a decorative “Play proof” control.

The app presents one dominant job per state. Desktop intentionally uses available width; mobile protects a single sequence. Do not paste the website composition into app routes or stretch a mobile card stack across desktop.

## Binding feedback and corrections

These corrections from product review remain part of the specification:

1. Preserve the complete product idea; sequence it professionally instead of reducing it to one prompt or rushing every feature.
2. Design and specify the app before trying to build the whole system.
3. Build page by page with complete states, security, workflow, accessibility, performance, and testing.
4. The public first impression must show the app and its consequence—not a random shape or generic AI decoration.
5. Hero text must be proportional and leave room for meaningful product imagery.
6. Keep the stronger public V6 direction; do not replace it with a weaker “polish” pass.
7. Desktop must be materially broader than mobile, with purposeful evidence/work regions.
8. Motion, imagery, and interaction must explain ProofMode rather than advertise implementation tools.
9. Avoid vibe-coded card grids. Decoration never substitutes for hierarchy, behavior, trust boundaries, or recovery.
10. Keep every unavailable installer, update, account, AI, payment, progress, or future feature honest.
11. Keep canonical specifications and handoff records in `main` so another advanced agent can continue without chat context.
12. Remove competition from the user experience and roadmap.

## Motivation without competition

Use mastery and clarity:

- a visible next lesson;
- completed real behaviors, not vague XP;
- a private replay that highlights one strong action and one improvement;
- gentle pace recommendations that never punish missed days;
- learner-controlled goals and comfort options;
- meaningful completion moments without comparison;
- local/private progress until identity and sync are deliberately approved.

## Current non-goals

No public profile, follower graph, direct messaging, open feed, employer assessment, hiring recommendation, intelligence or employability score, proctored certification, arbitrary browser execution, direct browser AI provider, client-authoritative result, fake installer/updater, fake checkout, unreviewed generated curriculum, competition, or cross-user comparison.
